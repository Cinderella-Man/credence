### The count that actually matters

## 5. Confidence and limits

**Strongest.** Every disposition that turns on "does this implementation work" rests on executed probes against live beams on Elixir 1.20.2 / OTP 29 — real compiler diagnostics, real dispatch over the discovered rule set, the rejected module loaded and its `fix/2` run on adversarial input. The three structural claims underwriting this analysis were re-verified directly: `lib/semantic.ex:191` is `Enum.find` with no fall-through; `lib/syntax.ex:70-83` is an unconditional reduce with no per-rule revert; `lib/pattern/rule.ex:5-6` bans warn-only rules.

**Weakest, in order.**

1. **The reconciliation pass read pre-overturn labels.** At least 5 rules it analyses as `salvage-small-fix` had already been overturned to `rebuild-later`. Two of its corrections collide head-on with the overturn pass: `no_private_fn_in_timer_mfa` (reconciliation: salvage→delete-duplicate; overturn: →rebuild-later) and `no_mapset_member_in_guard` (reconciliation: salvage→delete-duplicate; overturn: →rebuild-later). The *substance* agrees in both cases — do not build these standalone, keep the superset — but the labels must be reconciled before the per-rule table is authoritative. **Spot-check these two rows first.**

2. **81 delete verdicts received single-pass review only.** Of the 61 verdicts that *were* adversarially probed, 72% were overturned. If the unchecked deletes carry anything like that error rate, expect roughly 10–15 to be wrong. The asymmetry is deliberate and I would keep it: a wrong delete loses an observation that is already banked in the catalogue; a wrong build costs a person-week. But it means the delete pile is *not* verified to the standard of the work-creating pile, and "80.4% delete" should be read as a floor on correctness, not a measurement.
